# Demos: concept

What Demos is meant to feel like and how it behaves. The technical side is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## The feeling: you are talking to "the World"

The AI has no name or personality of its own. It *is* everyone else. So it speaks as **"we"**, never "I think".

> "Most of us in Milan think rent is the biggest problem. But a growing group of us, about 18%, says the real problem is wages, not rent. Want to hear why?"

It can also **speak as one group**, built only from what real people in that group said:

> "Let me speak as the people who disagree with you. We think..."

When there are not enough real users, it says so clearly: "Not many of us have talked about this yet. Here is what studies and polls say."

Identity: strong authentication for everyone, so no bots and no spam. Users can stay anonymous to other users if they prefer.

## Only three screens

The app stays really simple. Voice input works everywhere.

| Screen | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Talk** (home) | One big mic button and the conversation. Nothing else. You talk, the World answers. |
| **Map** | A concept map of all topics, not a geographic one. Bubble size shows how many people are in it. Color shows the gap: red is divided, green is agreement. Lines connect topics that affect each other. Geography is a filter. Tap a bubble and the World starts talking to you about it. |
| **You** | Your "mind profile": what you believe, how it changed over time, bridges you built, agreements you are part of, and your impact. |

## The World is proactive

When you open the app, the World often speaks first.

- **News for you:** "Since we last talked, 3,000 people joined the rent topic. One new argument goes against what you said. Want to hear it?"
- **It needs you:** "People who think like you haven't answered this argument yet. Can you help?" You become someone the World asks for help, not just a user.
- **Close to agreement:** "We are very close to an agreement on bike lanes in your area. Only people like you are still unsure. What's missing for you?"
- **Daily question:** one short question a day, to keep the map alive.

## How a conversation goes

The World moves between five modes, naturally, without you noticing.

1. **Listen:** asks questions, lets you explain.
2. **Mirror:** "So you agree with A, partly with B, and you're unsure about C. Right?" You correct it.
3. **Challenge:** brings the strongest argument from the other side. This is the "you are wrong" moment, but always from real people, never from the AI itself.
4. **Inform:** facts and data, always with a source, clearly labeled as data and not opinion.
5. **Bridge:** "Here is something both groups might accept. Would you?"

There is no classic voting. The World understands your position from how you talk. Opinions are fluid: when you change your mind, the numbers change automatically.

## Agreements

When an idea gets support across groups and keeps it for a while, it becomes an **Agreement**. It is shown as a simple card:

> "Milan should limit short-term rentals in the center, with exceptions for residents."
> Supported by 71% of renters, 58% of owners, 64% overall.

The card is live. Numbers keep moving. You can share it, send it to the city council, or use it as a petition. This is the moment where the app touches the real world.

## Why people come back

People accept hearing "you are wrong" because finding agreement changes their world, even by 1%.

- **Your words travel.** Maybe the strongest motive. "Your argument was heard by 4,812 people. 312 of them changed their view." You shaped the world, even anonymously.
- **Bridge builder:** reputation only from ideas that both sides accept. Convincing your own side gives nothing.
- **Open mind:** changing your view after a good argument is shown with pride, not shame.
- **Impact:** the agreements you helped build and what happened after.

## The World's honesty rules

These rules are what make people trust it.

- It never invents people or opinions.
- It always says if something comes from users or from data.
- It always shows real numbers.
- It never gives its own opinion on values.
- It gives every side its strongest version, even the side you hate.
- It says "we don't know" when we don't know.

## First open

Verify identity. Then a black screen and one sentence:

> "Hi. We are 1.2 million people. What's on your mind?"

The number here is an example. The app always shows the real count, however small.

No forms, no topic picking. You just talk, and the World figures out where you fit.
